Purpose

The purpose of this curriculum is to build mathematical fluency for scientific reasoning. Rather than learning formulas in isolation, the learner develops the ability to measure, calculate, recognize mathematical relationships, solve problems, and interpret scientific data.

Drawing on earlier work with negative numbers, decimals, algebra, coordinate geometry, special triangles, and trigonometry, the series is organized into four sequential courses, with five reference infographics supporting the topics. It suits independent study or structured instruction, and it makes no assumption that the learner is a child.

Materials. Scientific calculator with degree/radian modes, graph paper or graphing software, ruler, protractor, simple measuring tools, notebook, and the five accompanying online reference infographics.

How each week works

Each week contains two structured lessons, independent problem practice, a worked scientific application, and a brief assessment. The series begins with arithmetic fluency and culminates in multistep scientific problems involving algebra, graphs, and triangles.

Courses 1, 2, and 3 each run for 4 weeks (8 lessons); Course 4 runs for 8 weeks (16 lessons). Together, they cover 20 weeks and 40 lessons. Each week includes two 60- to 75-minute lessons, plus three short independent practice sessions. All pacing is adjustable after the diagnostic assessment.

The lesson cycle

  1. Recall (10 min) short cumulative arithmetic and notation drill.
  2. Demonstrate (15 min) model a worked example and narrate each mathematical decision.
  3. Practice (20–25 min) solve supported then independent problems; emphasize signs, substitutions, and showing steps.
  4. Apply (15–20 min) solve a measurement, graph, chemistry, physics, astronomy, or other science problem.
  5. Exit check (5 min) one procedural item plus one conceptual 'why' item.

Error-checking routine: Set up → solve one step → check sign → inspect units → estimate → verify.

Learning outcomes

By the end of all four courses, a learner should be able to:

  1. Perform operations with integers, signed fractions, and decimals, with accurate use of parentheses, absolute value, and order of operations.
  2. Calculate ratios, proportions, percents, rates, and unit conversions, and assess the reasonableness of scientific quantities.
  3. Apply integer and fractional exponents, square roots, radicals, and scientific notation.
  4. Simplify expressions, solve linear equations, rearrange scientific formulas, and verify solutions through substitution.
  5. Plot and interpret coordinates, slope, intercepts, and basic linear, quadratic, exponential, and periodic relationships.
  6. Use the Pythagorean theorem, special right-triangle ratios, the six trigonometric functions when appropriate, inverse trigonometry, degrees, radians, and the unit circle.
  7. Identify whether a triangle is right, acute, or obtuse; select the appropriate method from right-triangle methods, SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, or SSA; and address the SSA ambiguous case.
  8. Apply the Law of Sines, Law of Cosines, and appropriate triangle area formulas in scientifically grounded problems.
  9. Document steps, units, precision, checks, and interpretations clearly enough that another person can reproduce the result.

Assessments and mastery

  • Starting diagnostic. A brief skills check at the start of each course confirms readiness and identifies review needs; diagnostic only, not graded.
  • Each week. 12–20 problems balanced between new content and cumulative review; at least two interpretation/explanation problems.
  • Progress checks. Brief weekly checks and a trigonometry check at sequence Week 16, with error analysis and a retest after corrections.
  • Courses 1, 2, and 3 completion. Assessments at sequence weeks 4, 8, and 12 consolidate each course’s skills.
  • Course 4 completion (sequence Week 20). Cumulative quantitative assessment and science application capstone.
  • Suggested mastery threshold. at least 85% on each course’s targeted skills, plus correct unit handling and a valid independent explanation of the central method. This is an instructional recommendation, not a standardized external benchmark.

Main formulas

  • Right triangle: a² + b² = c² (c is hypotenuse).
  • Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C.
  • Cosine rule: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C (with corresponding permutations).
  • Trigonometric area: K = ½ab sin C.
  • Heron: s = (a + b + c)/2; K = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)].
  • Unit circle: (x, y) = (cos θ, sin θ); sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

After the four-course series

For deeper chemistry, physics, statistics, and research readiness, continue with: logarithms (pH and decibels); uncertainty propagation; probability and descriptive statistics; algebraic systems; vector notation and components; polynomial and rational functions; and introductory limits/calculus.

Accuracy note: Trigonometric formulas must use a consistent calculator angle mode; scientific reporting must not imply more measurement precision than the inputs support.
