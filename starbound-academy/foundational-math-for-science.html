Foundational Mathematics for Science

A complete learning pathway from elementary arithmetic through high school algebra, geometry, and trigonometry.

  • 20 weeks
  • 40 lessons
  • 5 units
  • 2 lessons a week

Purpose

The purpose of this curriculum is to build mathematical fluency for scientific reasoning. Rather than learning formulas in isolation, the learner develops the ability to measure, calculate, recognize mathematical relationships, solve problems, and interpret scientific data.

Drawing on earlier work with negative numbers, decimals, algebra, coordinate geometry, special triangles, and trigonometry, the course is organized around five foundational units. It suits independent study or structured instruction, and it makes no assumption that the learner is a child.

Materials. Scientific calculator with degree/radian modes, graph paper or graphing software, ruler, protractor, simple measuring tools, notebook, and the five accompanying PNG reference infographics.

How each week works

Each week contains two structured lessons, independent problem practice, a worked scientific application, and a brief assessment. The course begins with arithmetic fluency and culminates in multistep scientific problems involving algebra, graphs, and triangles.

20 weeks; two 60- to 75-minute lessons per week (40 lessons), plus three short independent practice sessions each week. All pacing is adjustable after the diagnostic assessment.

The lesson cycle

  1. Recall (10 min) short cumulative arithmetic and notation drill.
  2. Demonstrate (15 min) model a worked example and narrate each mathematical decision.
  3. Practice (20–25 min) solve supported then independent problems; emphasize signs, substitutions, and showing steps.
  4. Apply (15–20 min) solve a measurement, graph, chemistry, physics, astronomy, or other science problem.
  5. Exit check (5 min) one procedural item plus one conceptual 'why' item.

Error-checking routine: Set up → solve one step → check sign → inspect units → estimate → verify.

Week-by-week curriculum

Open a unit to see its four weeks: two lessons, a science application, and the evidence of mastery for each week.

Unit 1 Numbers and Operations Weeks 1–4

Reference infographic: Numbers, Signs & Operations →

Week 1 Integers, negatives, and absolute value

Lesson A
Classify whole, integer, rational, and real numbers; place signed numbers on a number line; compare magnitude and direction.
Lesson B
Add and subtract signed integers; understand subtraction as adding the opposite; calculate absolute value as distance from zero.
Science application
Interpret temperatures, elevation changes, and measurements above/below a reference level.
Evidence of mastery
Explain why −7 − (−3) = −4, and verify with a number line.

Week 2 Multiplication, division, decimals, and order of operations

Lesson A
Multiply and divide signed values; distinguish (−3)² from −3²; solve multistep expressions.
Lesson B
Place-value alignment, decimal operations, estimation, and precision of measurements.
Science application
Signed rates of change and arithmetic with laboratory measurements.
Evidence of mastery
Complete mixed operations with signs correctly and justify magnitude by estimation.

Week 3 Fractions, mixed numbers, ratios, and proportions

Lesson A
Equivalent fractions, simplification, common denominators, fraction operations, and mixed/improper conversions.
Lesson B
Unit rates, ratios, proportions, percent conversion, and percent change.
Science application
Sample proportions, map scales, and rate calculations.
Evidence of mastery
Solve proportional relationships and show cancellation of common factors.

Week 4 Measurement, conversions, and Unit 1 synthesis

Lesson A
Metric prefixes, powers of 10, unit-conversion chains, and interpreting units in multiplication/division.
Lesson B
Mixed arithmetic science problems; unit assessment and corrections.
Science application
Convert length, time, area, and speed using explicit unit factors.
Evidence of mastery
Produce 250 cm from 2.5 m using 2.5 m × (100 cm / 1 m), not an unexplained numerical rule.
Unit 2 Powers, Roots, and Algebra Weeks 5–8

Reference infographic: Powers, Roots & Algebra →

Week 5 Exponents and square roots

Lesson A
Repeated multiplication, exponent laws, zero and negative exponents; powers of ten.
Lesson B
Perfect squares, principal square root, absolute value in √(x²), and simplifying radicals.
Science application
Area scaling, inverse-square relationships (conceptual), and squared quantities.
Evidence of mastery
Identify when a proposed square-root simplification is invalid.

Week 6 Scientific notation and scale

Lesson A
Write numbers in a × 10ⁿ form; multiply and divide values with powers of ten.
Lesson B
Work with large/small scientific quantities, significant figures (introduction), and estimation.
Science application
Molecular scales, astronomical distances, and population counts.
Evidence of mastery
Correctly compare 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ and 4.5 × 10⁻³.

Week 7 Expressions and equations

Lesson A
Coefficients, variables, constants, distributive property, and combining like terms with signed coefficients.
Lesson B
One- and two-step linear equations, equations with variables on both sides, and checking by substitution.
Science application
Solve an unknown measurement in a simple scientific model.
Evidence of mastery
Justify each inverse operation and correctly classify an equation as one, none, or infinitely many solutions.

Week 8 Rearranging scientific formulas

Lesson A
Isolate any one variable in v = d/t, F = ma, and ρ = m/V; maintain units and nonzero-denominator assumptions.
Lesson B
Inequalities and interval meaning; Unit 2 mixed assessment.
Science application
Calculate time from speed/distance and mass from density/volume.
Evidence of mastery
Derive t = d/v when v ≠ 0 and check by substitution.
Unit 3 Functions, Graphs, and Measurement Weeks 9–12

Reference infographic: Graphs, Functions & Units →

Week 9 Cartesian coordinates and slope

Lesson A
Plot ordered pairs in four quadrants, identify intercepts, and interpret scales.
Lesson B
Slope as rise/run and Δy/Δx; positive, negative, zero, and undefined slope.
Science application
Estimate average velocity from a position-time graph.
Evidence of mastery
Compute slope with a consistent subtraction order.

Week 10 Lines and coordinate geometry

Lesson A
Slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard forms; move between forms.
Lesson B
Distance and midpoint formulas; graph a linear relation with units.
Science application
Interpolate values and estimate displacement between positions.
Evidence of mastery
Show equivalence between forms and check two points on the same line.

Week 11 Functions and scientific patterns

Lesson A
Identify input/output, domain/range, graph transformations, linear versus quadratic models.
Lesson B
Introductory exponential change; recognize periodic data; optional introduction to logarithmic scales.
Science application
Compare constant-speed, accelerating, growth/decay, and cyclic patterns.
Evidence of mastery
Explain which model is plausible for a given set of observations, without implying that correlation proves causation.

Week 12 Dimensional analysis and data reasoning

Lesson A
Compound units, scientific equations, dimensional consistency, density, and rate calculations.
Lesson B
Plot a small data set, fit/estimate a reasonable line, discuss measurement error and significant figures; Unit 3 assessment.
Science application
Interpret an experiment measuring travel distance over time.
Evidence of mastery
Label both graph axes, attach units, and interpret slope in context.
Unit 4 Trigonometry and the Unit Circle Weeks 13–16

Reference infographic: Trigonometry & the Unit Circle →

Week 13 Triangle geometry and Pythagorean reasoning

Lesson A
Angles, triangle sum, triangle inequality, right-triangle vocabulary, and the Pythagorean theorem.
Lesson B
Exact ratios for 45°–45°–90° and 30°–60°–90° triangles; square-root simplification.
Science application
Geometric components of a rectangular field or inclined structure.
Evidence of mastery
Derive a diagonal from perpendicular components.

Week 14 Sine, cosine, tangent, and inverse trig

Lesson A
Label opposite/adjacent/hypotenuse relative to a specified angle; calculate sin, cos, tan.
Lesson B
Inverse trig for a missing angle; angle of elevation/depression; choose the appropriate trig function.
Science application
Determine unknown heights or distances from a measured angle.
Evidence of mastery
Distinguish a ratio problem from an inverse-ratio problem and set calculator mode correctly.

Week 15 Radians and unit-circle values

Lesson A
Degrees ↔ radians, full rotation, quadrants, reference angles, exact unit-circle coordinates (cos θ, sin θ).
Lesson B
Evaluate sine and cosine at reference and quadrant angles; identify sign patterns and tangent where defined.
Science application
Angular position and rotational motion.
Evidence of mastery
Explain why sin 150° = 1/2 and cos 150° = −√3/2.

Week 16 Trigonometric identities and periodic models

Lesson A
Unit circle identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1; use tan θ = sin θ/cos θ when cos θ ≠ 0.
Lesson B
Sketch sine and cosine waves; interpret amplitude, period, and phase conceptually; Unit 4 assessment.
Science application
Oscillation, wave motion, and recurring signals.
Evidence of mastery
Connect an angle to its circle coordinates and corresponding periodic graph values.
Unit 5 Solving Triangles Weeks 17–20

Reference infographic: Solve Any Triangle →

Week 17 Right-triangle choice and Law of Sines

Lesson A
Decision process for right triangles; combine the Pythagorean theorem, trig ratios, and inverse trig.
Lesson B
Law of Sines for AAS and ASA configurations, after finding the third angle.
Science application
Measured angle-and-baseline surveys.
Evidence of mastery
Identify the matching side-angle pairs (a, A), (b, B), (c, C).

Week 18 Law of Cosines and the SSA ambiguous case

Lesson A
Use Law of Cosines for SAS or SSS and then complete the triangle.
Lesson B
Use Law of Sines with SSA and test all possible valid angle configurations (0, 1, or 2 solutions); check domain and 180° sum.
Science application
Solve distance measurements from two known lengths and an included angle.
Evidence of mastery
Determine why one inverse-sine result may not represent the only valid triangle.

Week 19 Triangle area and applications

Lesson A
Area = ½bh; area = ½ab sin C; Heron's formula given SSS.
Lesson B
Apply triangle decomposition, vector components, and geometry in physical contexts.
Science application
Plan-area estimates, forces on an incline, and surveying.
Evidence of mastery
Match area formula to available data and maintain square units.

Week 20 Cumulative science capstone

Lesson A
Integrative problem solving: signed measurements, decimal/fraction calculations, conversions, exponents, graph slope, and triangles.
Lesson B
Capstone presentation/explanation and cumulative assessment; reflect on corrected misconceptions.
Investigation option
Measure a baseline and angles, solve a triangle, estimate the target distance/height, compare with an independent measurement, and graph repeated readings. Explain the assumptions, units, and uncertainty.
Evidence of mastery
Reproducible calculations, clearly labeled diagrams, correct method choice, and an evidence-based interpretation.

Learning outcomes

By the end, a learner should be able to:

  1. Perform operations with integers, signed fractions, and decimals, with accurate use of parentheses, absolute value, and order of operations.
  2. Calculate ratios, proportions, percents, rates, and unit conversions, and assess the reasonableness of scientific quantities.
  3. Apply integer and fractional exponents, square roots, radicals, and scientific notation.
  4. Simplify expressions, solve linear equations, rearrange scientific formulas, and verify solutions through substitution.
  5. Plot and interpret coordinates, slope, intercepts, and basic linear, quadratic, exponential, and periodic relationships.
  6. Use the Pythagorean theorem, special right-triangle ratios, the six trigonometric functions when appropriate, inverse trigonometry, degrees, radians, and the unit circle.
  7. Identify whether a triangle is right, acute, or obtuse; select the appropriate method from right-triangle methods, SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, or SSA; and address the SSA ambiguous case.
  8. Apply the Law of Sines, Law of Cosines, and appropriate triangle area formulas in scientifically grounded problems.
  9. Document steps, units, precision, checks, and interpretations clearly enough that another person can reproduce the result.

Assessments and mastery

  • Week 1 diagnostic. 25 mixed items from integers through unit-circle recognition; diagnostic only, not graded.
  • Each week. 12–20 problems balanced between new content and cumulative review; at least two interpretation/explanation problems.
  • Weeks 4, 8, 12, 16. mixed-skill unit assessments with error analysis and a retest after corrections.
  • Week 20. cumulative quantitative assessment and science application capstone.
  • Suggested mastery threshold. at least 85% on each unit's targeted skills, plus correct unit handling and a valid independent explanation of the central method. This is an instructional recommendation, not a standardized external benchmark.

Main formulas

  • Right triangle: a² + b² = c² (c is hypotenuse).
  • Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C.
  • Cosine rule: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C (with corresponding permutations).
  • Trigonometric area: K = ½ab sin C.
  • Heron: s = (a + b + c)/2; K = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)].
  • Unit circle: (x, y) = (cos θ, sin θ); sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

After Week 20

For deeper chemistry, physics, statistics, and research readiness, continue with: logarithms (pH and decibels); uncertainty propagation; probability and descriptive statistics; algebraic systems; vector notation and components; polynomial and rational functions; and introductory limits/calculus.

Accuracy note: Trigonometric formulas must use a consistent calculator angle mode; scientific reporting must not imply more measurement precision than the inputs support.
