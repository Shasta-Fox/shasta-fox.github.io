Aloha ʻĀina ʻAi

Nourishing every Hawaiʻi Island home with fresh, local food: channeling farm abundance and teaching the skills to turn harvests into healthy meals.

Status: in formation. Aloha ʻĀina ʻAi is not yet incorporated and does not yet have 501(c)(3) status. This page is its prospectus: the plan and the steps to launch.

The plan

Aloha ʻĀina ʻAi is a planned community nonprofit focused on food sovereignty in Puna. It pairs two kinds of work: getting surplus farm produce to Puna households, and teaching hands-on cooking so people can turn that produce into meals. Shasta Fox and Chef Anthony Rinaldi are its founders.

Food rescue

Puna farms often grow more than they sell. The plan is to collect that surplus and bring it to households that need it.

Cooking skills

Free, hands-on classes so families can turn a box of vegetables into healthy meals. The first is a pilot class led by Chef Rinaldi.

Community food

Planned first-year projects include a community fridge and a holiday food drive.

Steps to launch

What has to be in place before Aloha ʻĀina ʻAi can run programs and apply for grants.

  • Hawaiʻi incorporation and an IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter
  • A board, with bylaws and conflict-of-interest, whistleblower, and records policies
  • A bookkeeping system that can be audited
  • A balanced first-year budget and a program logic model
  • Outcome measures: households served, pounds of food rescued, and cooking confidence
  • Partnership agreements with at least 3 Puna farmers' markets and 2 anchor farms
  • Insurance: general liability, auto, and volunteer accident
  • Privacy and photo-release rules for participants

Get involved

We are looking for Puna farmers with extra produce, volunteers, community partners, and founding board members: a chair, a secretary, and a treasurer.
