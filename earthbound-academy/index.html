Foundational Culinary Arts 101

Build the habits of a confident cook. Learn one technique at a time, understand why it works, and practice it with ingredients you can find in Puna.

Teacher
Chef Anthony “Tony” Rinaldi
Format
Six weeks, 2.5 hours a week · 15 hours
Level
Beginners welcome; no prerequisite
Group
4–6 people
Cost
$780 per person, ingredients included
Explore Foundational Culinary Arts 101

Week 1 · A safe, organized kitchen

Set up your station, read a recipe, measure accurately, handle food safely, and practice a secure knife grip and even vegetable cuts. Cook a simple vegetable sauté.

Week 2 · Stock, soup, and seasoning

Build a vegetable stock and a seasonal soup. Practice simmering, straining, and balancing salt, acidity, and texture; plan safe cooling and storage.

Week 3 · Heat and vegetables

Compare blanching, steaming, roasting, and sautéing. Cook vegetables and a grain side, and explain how size, moisture, and pan heat change the result.

Week 4 · Eggs and everyday sauces

Practice eggs, a roux-based béchamel, and a vinaigrette. Learn thickening and emulsification, then use your sauce in a simple composed dish.

Week 5 · Searing, roasting, and pan sauce

Cook a chicken or plant-based main with a seasonal side. Practice browning, checking doneness, resting, deglazing, and timing two components.

Week 6 · Cook with confidence

Plan and cook a main, side, and sauce using the skills from class. Chef Tony gives feedback on organization, knife control, seasoning, doneness, and timing.

What you can do afterward

Organize a station, choose a cooking method, build a basic sauce, and serve a balanced meal. Continue into Culinary Arts 102.

Foundational Culinary Arts 102

Bring the foundations together in a French-inspired kitchen. Refine sauces, handle gentle and slow cooking, bake a simple dessert, and serve a complete menu.

Teacher
Chef Anthony “Tony” Rinaldi
Format
Six weeks, 2.5 hours a week · 15 hours
Level
Complete 101, or discuss equivalent kitchen experience with Chef Tony
Group
4–6 people
Cost
$780 per person, ingredients included
Explore Foundational Culinary Arts 102

Week 1 · Stocks into sauces

Review stock quality, then make velouté and a reduced pan sauce. Adjust body and seasoning and pair a sauce with the food it supports.

Week 2 · Emulsions with confidence

Practice hollandaise and beurre blanc, manage temperature, and troubleshoot separation. Serve with vegetables or eggs.

Week 3 · Fish and gentle cooking

Practice poaching and cooking fish in parchment, with a vegetable alternative. Match cooking time to thickness and coordinate a sauce and garnish.

Week 4 · Braises and a bistro plate

Build flavor by browning, deglazing, and braising. Use a prepared batch for tasting while starting your own; pair it with a potato preparation.

Week 5 · The baker’s foundations

Make shortcrust pastry and a seasonal fruit tart. Practice weighing, keeping dough cool, resting, rolling, and judging a fully baked base.

Week 6 · Your three-course table

Work from a prep schedule to serve a starter, main, and dessert. Review flavor, texture, portioning, plating, and timing with Chef Tony.

What you can do afterward

Plan and coordinate a three-course meal, refine sauces, and troubleshoot common cooking and pastry problems.

Format

Group
4–6 people

See what we cover

Week 1 · Flour, water, salt, yeast

Weigh in grams, use baker’s percentages, and mix a simple lean dough.

Week 2 · Build strength

Practice kneading and folds, and learn when dough needs more time rather than more flour.

Week 3 · Read fermentation

Judge bulk fermentation and proofing by feel and appearance; adjust your plan for a warm kitchen.

Week 4 · Shape and bake

Shape rolls and a loaf, practice scoring, and compare crust and crumb after cooling.

Week 5 · Meet sourdough

Learn starter feeding and readiness. Work with prepared dough to see a fermentation cycle that extends beyond class.

Week 6 · Your signature loaf

Plan, shape, and bake a loaf; diagnose crumb and crust with Chef Tony and build a home baking schedule.

Flatbread in a Day

Flour on your hands, warm bread on the table. Spend one day with Chef Tony mixing, rolling, stretching, and cooking three versatile flatbreads.

Teacher
Chef Anthony “Tony” Rinaldi
Format
One day · 5 hours, including a shared tasting
Level
Beginners welcome; no prerequisite
Group
4–6 people
Cost
$220 per person, ingredients included
Explore Flatbread in a Day

First hour · Understand your dough

Weigh ingredients, compare flour and hydration, and mix an unleavened dough and a yeasted dough. Learn how resting makes dough easier to roll.

Second hour · Roll and cook

Make soft skillet flatbreads. Practice even thickness, pan heat, and keeping cooked breads supple.

Third hour · Stretch and bake

Shape pita-style breads and explore how heat and thickness help a pocket form. Use prepared dough where fermentation needs more time.

Fourth hour · Top and share

Make herb-topped flatbreads with seasonal vegetables and a dip. Taste together and compare softness, blistering, and crispness.

Final hour · Repeat it at home

Practice your shaping again, troubleshoot dense or dry bread, and review cooling, storage, freezing, and reheating.

What you can do afterward

Mix and shape flatbread dough, control skillet or oven heat, and adapt toppings for an everyday meal.

Get involved

East Side Catering Academy grew out of Aloha ʻĀina ʻe mālama iā Puna, a community food project in Puna directed by Shasta Fox. Its goal: get fresh food from Puna farms into Puna homes, and teach the skills to turn a harvest into a meal.

Food from Puna farms

Aloha ʻĀina ʻe mālama iā Puna starts with what Puna farms already grow, and it aims to get more of that food into Puna kitchens.

Food that would go to waste

Puna farms often grow more than they can sell. Aloha ʻĀina ʻe mālama iā Puna plans to collect that surplus and bring it to households that need it.

Skills to cook it

Fresh produce helps most when people know what to do with it. Aloha ʻĀina ʻe mālama iā Puna plans free, hands-on cooking classes so families can turn a box of vegetables into healthy meals.

Help start the nonprofit

Aloha ʻĀina ʻe mālama iā Puna is a nonprofit in formation. It is not incorporated yet. We are looking for Puna farmers with extra produce, volunteers, community partners, and founding board members.
